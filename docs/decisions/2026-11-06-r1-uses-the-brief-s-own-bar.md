# R1 uses the brief's own bar, with a five-dollar floor

**6 November 2026.** At the owner's request ("please somehow make it work,
without changing anything on the Google Ads account, I want some
suggestions").

## Why

The client's brief defines a losing search: "Minimum data to call a loser:
1.5x target cost per lead in spend, or 100 clicks." PPCWay added a $20 floor on
top, so that an account with a very low target would not start excluding
searches that cost pennies.

On a small account that floor became the whole test. PDFezy spends about $5 a
day and its cost per lead is about $2.70. The brief's bar there is about $4.
Yet no search could be called wasted until it alone had spent $20 in 30 days,
and at that spend almost none ever does. Otto's inbox stayed empty while
searches that brought nobody kept costing money.

## The change

- **The floor is now $5**, in the account's own currency. The brief's bar,
  1.5× the target cost per lead or 100 clicks, decides above that. A search
  that cost pennies is still never offered.
- **R1 is now version 2.** Every proposal records the version of the rule
  that wrote it, so a card written under the $20 floor still explains itself
  by that floor.
- **When nothing is found, the inbox says what was checked:** how many
  searches, how many brought a lead, the bar, and the search that came
  nearest to it. "We looked and found nothing" was true but showed no work.

Nothing else about R1 moves. It is still an exact-match exclusion that stops
one search, it can be undone for 30 days, and it is never offered for the
business's own name. It still stands down on an account that counts no
enquiries (`2026-11-06-what-counts-as-a-lead.md`).
