What the Google Ads API
lets PPCWay do

Every part of the Google Ads API that becomes available once the developer token is raised to Basic or Standard access, written out in full and sorted into two plain groups: the calls that only look at an account, and the calls that change something in it.

The distinction matters more than any technical detail in this document. Reading an account is how an audit is produced. Changing one is how money gets spent. PPCWay does the first today, and will only ever do the second with the account owner's explicit approval of the specific change, recorded before it is applied.

How to read this document

The Google Ads API is not a long list of web addresses, the way most APIs are. It has a very small number of addresses and a very large number of things you can ask for at each one. Three facts make the rest of this document straightforward.

1. There is one address for reading, and it answers hundreds of questions

Everything an account can tell you — spend, clicks, keywords, search terms, ads, policy decisions, settings, history — comes back from a single endpoint:

POST /v25/customers/{customerId}/googleAds:search

You send it a query written in Google Ads Query Language, naming which resource you want (for example search_term_view) and which fields of it. So the list of "read endpoints" in this document is really a list of resources you can ask that one endpoint about. None of them can alter anything; a query is a question.

2. Changing something is a different kind of call, and is named plainly

Each kind of thing you can change has its own address ending in :mutate, and each one accepts exactly three operations: create, update and remove.

POST /v25/customers/{customerId}/campaignBudgets:mutate

There is no way to change an account by accident through a read call, and no way to read through a mutate call. The two are separate by design, which is what makes the promise in this document checkable rather than rhetorical.

3. The access level decides which services are allowed, not which reports

Raising the token to Basic does not unlock extra reports. Every report listed in the next section is available at any level — against test accounts at Test level, and against real accounts from Explorer upwards. What the level governs is the daily volume, and whether a handful of sensitive services may be called at all.

The four access levels, in Google's own numbers

where the token is today		 Test accounts only. Real accounts are refused outright. 15,000 operations a day All production data. This is why PPCWay cannot read a live account yet.

what we are asking for		 Test and real accounts 15,000 a day on both Nothing from the list above. Brand verification is required to be granted it.

What one "operation" means. A whole report counts as one operation, however many rows come back: Google states that a Search or SearchStream request "counts as one operation… irrespective of the number of batches". Changes are counted one by one instead — editing forty keywords is forty operations.

So a PPCWay audit, which runs five reports, spends five of the day's allowance per merchant. At Basic access that is roughly three thousand audits a day before the limit is anywhere in sight.

Part one — View only reading an account

Nothing in this part can alter an account. Every item below is a report requested through googleAds:search. They are the same reports the account owner sees in the Google Ads interface, read programmatically instead of by eye.

Part two — Changes the account writing to it

Everything in this part alters a live advertising account, and most of it can affect spending within minutes. PPCWay calls none of it today. When that changes, no call in this part will ever be made without the account owner approving that specific change first, and every one of them will be written to an append-only record before it is applied, so that what was changed, when, and on whose authority can always be answered.

How a change is made

Each address below accepts a list of operations, and each operation is one of three things: create something new, update something that exists (naming exactly which fields may change), or remove it. Google permits up to 10,000 operations in a single request, or 20,000 for ad group criteria, and offers a "partial failure" mode in which valid operations proceed and invalid ones are reported back rather than taking the whole request down.

Why this part matters to the product. Without it, PPCWay can tell a business what is wrong. With it, PPCWay can tell them what it is worth in pounds and pence — which is the difference between a report that is read and a report that is acted on.

What PPCWay calls today

The whole audit, as deployed, is five read queries and one listing call per merchant. Every one of them is in part one.

QueryWhat the merchant is shown
customer"You spent $47.18 on Google Ads in the last thirty days. That bought 603 clicks at $0.08 each."
search_term_view"$7.00 went to searches that brought no enquiry", with the searches listed.
keyword_view"Google scores four of your keywords at five or less, and they took $12.40."
campaign"Emergency plumbing missed 34% of its searches because the budget ran out."
ad_group_ad"Two of your ads are disapproved, so they are not being shown at all."
customers:listAccessibleCustomersLets the owner choose which of their accounts we read.

That is six operations out of a daily allowance of fifteen thousand. The request for Basic access is not about volume; it is simply that at Test level Google refuses all six against a real account.

What PPCWay promises about part two

  • Nothing is changed without a specific yes. Not a category of changes agreed once, but the individual change, described in plain words, approved before it happens.
  • Everything is written down before it is applied. Every automated change goes to an append-only record first — what, when, why, and on whose authority — and that record is the merchant's to read.
  • Every change passes a fixed set of safety checks in a fixed order before it reaches Google, including limits on how far a budget may move.
  • One door. A single service in the system holds the Google credentials and is the only thing permitted to write. Nothing else can reach the account, by construction.
  • The owner can revoke at any time, from their own Google account, without asking us. The connection stops working immediately.
