Phase 1’s exit gate, counted rather than claimed

The requirements will not let PPCway write to anybody’s account until five accounts have synced cleanly for seven consecutive days. PPCway counts this itself and reports it, so the gate cannot be declared passed because nobody checked. Read from the live system today:

0 of the 5 accounts the gate asks for have 7 clean days. It is running on 1 connected account. The gate is not passed.

That one account, Byte9, has 5 clean days out of the last 7 across 752 sync runs. It names its own two bad days: 13 October, when no sync ran, and 14 October, when a query asked Google for two fields it had renamed — since fixed. Byte9 alone reaches seven clean days on 22 October.

What this needs is four more Google Ads accounts to read, not more code. Any live account will do, and connecting one takes about a minute. Until there are five, the gate is arithmetic that cannot complete.

What a business owner can do today

app.ppcway.com

Get in, and connect their advertising

  • Sign up and sign in two ways: with Google, or with an email address and a password, with a confirmation link and a reset that both send real email.
  • Invite their own people, with five roles — owner, admin, member, expert, client viewer — enforced in the system, not just hidden in the interface.
  • Connect their Google Ads account by granting permission to Google directly. They choose which single account PPCway may read, and they can disconnect inside the product at any time, which destroys the stored permission immediately.

/home

See what their advertising did, in plain words

  • Leads, phone calls, form fills, money spent, clicks and views for the last 30 days, with a sentence before every number.
  • Each figure carries the change against the period before it — “up 18%”, “down 4%” — so a figure is never shown without saying whether it is better or worse than last time.
  • Needs you: a short list of real problems, in the owner’s language. “This campaign has spent $240 and brought no leads.” “An ad was refused by Google.”
  • Changed in Google Ads: anything edited directly in Google in the last seven days, so no change happens behind the product’s back.
  • A trend line beside each campaign, and the website’s Visibility Score over time.

/campaigns

Look into any campaign

  • Every campaign with its spend, leads, clicks and cost per lead, and the dates covered printed on the screen so the figure can be checked against Google Ads and will match.
  • Open one campaign for five tabs: overview, ad groups, keywords, ads, and the history of what changed and who changed it.
  • A campaign still learning says so, and counts the days until it has learned.

/reports

Get a report worth sending to somebody

  • Thirty days, ninety days, or this year against last year.
  • Every figure gets a verdict in words, not an arrow: a real change, normal variation, or too little data to tell yet. The verdict is calculated, so normal weekly noise is never sold as a win.
  • A definitions panel that explains each term in one sentence.
  • Share this report builds a private link that opens with no login and no account. It expires after fourteen days, can be revoked at any moment, and shows that one report and nothing else.

/tools/audit

Find out what is wrong with their website, repeatedly

  • Fourteen public checks on the Google listing, the website and the competition, with a real crawler, a real browser and a live mobile speed test. A score out of 100 and specific, fixable findings.
  • It now runs again every night by itself, so the score is current rather than a snapshot of the day somebody asked.
  • Score over time, and what changed since last time — what was fixed, and what broke.
  • Page by page: the actual pages the ads send people to are opened and judged. Is there a form, a tappable phone number, a clear button? How fast does it load on a phone?
  • The fix list can be sent to a web developer as a link that needs no login.

by email

Hear from PPCway without logging in

  • Monday morning: a summary of the week — what was spent, how many leads, what changed, what needs attention.
  • When something is wrong: spend has suddenly doubled, leads have stopped, an ad was refused, our access to the account was lost, the sync keeps failing, or the website score dropped. Six conditions, and at most one email per issue per day, so the alerts stay worth opening.

Free tools, open to visitors who have not signed up

  • The website audit, a budget calculator, a cost-per-lead and ROAS calculator, and an ad copy checker.
  • Google’s own search volumes turned into a money line — “about 11,280 searches a month for what you do in Oakville, $18 to $53 a click”.
  • An audit run before signing up follows the person into their account afterwards.

What runs without anybody touching it

Six jobs run on a clock in Canada. All six are enabled and have been seen to work. This is the difference between a dashboard somebody has to refresh and a service that watches an account.

JobWhenWhat it does
Structure syncevery 15 minutes Notices campaigns, ad groups, keywords and ads added, changed or removed in Google Ads
Today’s figuresevery hour Brings today’s spend, clicks and leads up to date
Nightly catch-up03:15 Toronto Re-reads the last three days, because Google revises recent figures for a day or two
Website audit02:00 Toronto Re-audits every website in the system so the score stays current
Alertsevery hour Checks the six conditions above and emails only when one is genuinely met
The Monday postMondays 08:00 Toronto Sends the weekly summary. First send: Monday 26 October

The alerts job has run every hour today and sent nothing. That is the correct answer, not a failure: the connected account met none of the six conditions. A system that emails when there is nothing to say trains people to ignore it.

Proved against the live account, not assumed

Four things were run against production on 20 October rather than taken on trust:

  • The gate answers with real data, naming its own two bad days rather than rounding them away.
  • A full scheduled audit ran by itself: 11 findings, the search-demand figures, the account’s own four checks, and one real landing page opened and judged — a page taken from the live campaign that sends people there.
  • Both login-free links work, and an unknown link is refused in the same words as an expired one, so a wrong guess learns nothing.
  • Two faults the first live run found were fixed the same day: one website was queued for audit twice because it had been saved once with a tracking tag on the address and once without; and a finished audit read on its own reported no score while the same audit in a list reported a number.
