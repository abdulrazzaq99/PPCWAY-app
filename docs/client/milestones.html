Every requirement in the Developer Requirements document and the customer dashboard handoff, set against what is already built and running, with a date for each milestone and the reasoning behind it.

This is the two-month plan of 12 October, rewritten to four weeks at the client’s request. The scope has not been reduced; the rate has been doubled, to two milestones a week. Two things still cannot be compressed by working faster: Google’s reviews, and the soak periods the requirements themselves set as exit gates. Both are named and dated separately, so the plan does not pretend they are work.

What changed from the two-month plan

The plan sent on 12 October ran nine weeks to Friday 11 December, with a milestone a week and about a month of room in it. This revision removes that room and runs two milestones a week for four weeks. Three things make that a plan rather than a wish, and one thing makes it a risk.

What changedEffect on the dates
Week one is already banked Organisations, memberships and the five roles; email and password sign-up with verification; invitations; sessions that stay signed in — all deployed on 12 and 13 October, along with the transactional email that was week four’s. The four weeks below start from that, not from the old week one.
The dashboard is finished early Every screen now uses the client’s design, reading live data or saying plainly which week its machinery arrives. The old week three was partly a design week; it is now only a data week.
Google’s clock started on time Basic access was granted on 12 October and app verification was submitted the same day, so the longest external wait is already five days in rather than starting now.
The room is gone This is the honest cost. The two-month plan absorbed a slow decision or a Google query without moving the end date. This one cannot: if a risk on the last page lands, the fortnight after 6 November absorbs it, and the core four weeks hold.

The single biggest ask of the client is not money or scope — it is speed of answer. Two decisions that the old plan needed by 13 November are now needed by Friday 23 October: which change types may auto-apply, and the data residency question. They are written into the first morning of week three. A fortnight’s delay on either costs more than everything else on the risk page put together.

How four weeks is possible, and where it is tight

This is not a conventional estimate, because it is not a conventional pace. In four weeks the project has produced the audit engine, the Google Ads integration, the deployment, the domain, the client’s dashboard, the live account reading, roles and email — and the last three of those took a single day. The four weeks below assume that continues at two milestones a week rather than one.

What they do not assume is that anything outside the keyboard moves faster. Google reviews take the time Google takes. A seven-day sync with no mismatches takes seven days. Thirty days of pilot writes takes thirty days. Those are on a separate track, and they are why the build finishing on 6 November does not mean customers on the same day.

The four weeks

Friday 16 October 2026

Week 1 · The local mirror, and the connection finished

  • Campaigns, ad groups, keywords, ads and daily performance facts held in our own database, with the Google resource name and the version we last saw.
  • The sync: structure every fifteen minutes, performance hourly for today, and a nightly pass over the previous three days to catch late conversions. Manual refresh on demand.
  • External change detection: anything changed directly in Google Ads is noticed, recorded as "made in Google Ads", and does not get silently overwritten.
  • The connect flow in full: the handoff’s Get started screen, both paths, watching the first sync stream in, landing on a first look — and disconnecting from a screen, with the stored permission deleted.

Why it is first: every screen after this reads from the mirror rather than from Google, which is what makes the two-second dashboard in the requirements possible at all, and what the recommendation engine in week three reads.

Gate starts: seven days of clean sync on five internal accounts.

Friday 23 October 2026

Week 2 · The dashboard on our own data, and the audit as a living tool

  • Home against the mirror: the results strip with the change against the previous period, "needs you", the activity list, and the Visibility Score over time.
  • Campaigns and campaign detail finished: the per-row sparkline, the Learning chip counting its days, and all five tabs.
  • Reports: thirty days, ninety days and year on year, the significance test that decides "a real change" from "within the normal range", the definitions panel, and sharing by an expiring link.
  • The audit becomes a living tool: scheduled re-runs — website daily, listing weekly, ads with each sync — the score history with markers where fixes landed, "what changed since last time", and per-page checks on every page the ads send people to.
  • The post: the weekly plain-language summary every Monday, the alerts, and "send the fix list to my web person" with a link that needs no login. The provider itself is already live.

You can see at the end of the week: the handoff’s home screen with Byte9’s own numbers in it, loading from our database, and a Monday email about them.

Decisions needed by this Friday: auto-apply defaults, and data residency.

Friday 30 October 2026

Week 3 · Otto proposes, and the pipeline that will let it act

  • The recommendation engine: a daily pass over each account that turns findings into proposals — wasteful search terms, weak keywords, budget lost to a cap, a disapproved ad, a bidding strategy with enough data to change — each with a plain reason, the evidence, an expected impact and an undo plan.
  • The inbox and the cards from the handoff: the lifecycle bar, the evidence list, the before-and-after table, and the result when a change is measured.
  • The language model behind a server-side service, with prompt logging and policy filtering, writing words only. Every number in a card comes from the account, not the model.
  • Proposed change and applied change as first-class records: type, target, before, after, reason, evidence, risk, route, status — immutable once applied, which is what powers both the history and the undo.
  • Guardrails, checked before every write in a fixed order: daily and monthly spend caps, the largest budget move allowed in a day, blocked keywords, protected brand terms, and no changes inside the fourteen-day learning window except fixes.
  • The write path to Google: one service, batched mutates, partial-failure handling, a per-account queue with retries and backoff, and an alert at 80% of the daily quota.

Still nothing is applied. This is the week the product starts having opinions and gains the machinery to act on them — and the week to check the opinions are good ones before week four turns the key.

Exit gate: the client’s Google Ads consultant reviews a week of real recommendations for accuracy, starting the moment the first pass runs.

Friday 6 November 2026

Week 4 · Approving, undoing, editing, and the expert

  • The three routes: auto-applied and reported, one tap by the owner, and expert sign-off before the owner sees it — with the thresholds as settings, so an owner can tighten or loosen them within what their plan allows.
  • Undo: the toast with its six seconds, and thirty days to revert anything from the change history, with the revert itself logged as a change.
  • Editing from the dashboard: pause, resume, the budget preview card with its three options, a negative keyword in one click from a wasteful search term, and bulk actions.
  • The expert console: the queue of assigned accounts, sign-off with a note the customer sees, booked sessions, and hours logged against the plan allowance.
  • Chat with Otto: the tools that let it read the account, and a proposed change card that can only become a change through the same approval as everything else.

At the end of this week the product is complete for a business that already advertises: it audits, recommends, changes with approval, and undoes.

Gate starts: thirty days of pilot writes with zero guardrail breaches. Applied the same day: Standard API access, whose required features now exist.

The fortnight after, for the part that is not about existing advertisers

Phase 4 of the requirements serves businesses with no Google Ads account at all, and the plan machinery around it. None of it is needed by a customer who already advertises, which is why it sits outside the four weeks rather than inside them, where it would push everything else past the month.

Friday 13 November 2026

Week 5 · New advertisers

  • The PPCway manager account and account creation: a client account in the customer’s currency and time zone, the customer invited as admin so they own it, and the guided deep link into Google’s billing, with launch blocked until billing is active.
  • The campaign builder, all eight steps: goal, services, where and when, keywords with real estimates from Keyword Planner, ads with a live preview and a policy check, budget and bidding, tracking, then review and launch — autosaving at every step, and writing through week three’s pipeline rather than around it.
  • Learning mode: the fourteen to twenty-one day window, the day-3 and day-7 check-ins, and the rule that no bids move inside it.
Friday 20 November 2026

Week 6 · Plans, the phone, and everything found by using it

  • Plans, limits and billing: Starter, Growth and Agency with their account, spend and expert-hour limits enforced in the backend, the per-currency price book, the subscription, and the warnings at 80% and 100% of managed spend.
  • The phone layout, which the handoff designs separately: a tab bar, a floating Ask Otto, and approvals that work with a thumb.
  • Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA, localisation of currency and time zone with the interface ready for twelve languages, traces across the gateway and a status page.
  • A security and privacy pass: the data processing agreement, automated deletion within thirty days of a request, staff multi-factor sign-in, and the client secret rotated.
  • The handoff’s QA list: the same numbers on every screen for the same range, approve then undo restoring both the account and the badge counts, a budget rise of 50% routing to the expert, the builder surviving a reload, the streams recovering from a dropped connection, and every screen against its screenshot at 1440 pixels.

At the end of this week every requirement in both documents is written, tested and deployed — five weeks earlier than the two-month plan.

What the build cannot buy, however fast it goes

The requirements set exit gates that are measured in calendar days, and Google sets review times nobody can hurry. Both run behind the build rather than inside it, and they are the reason the core finishing on 6 November is not the same as opening the doors. Compressing the build from nine weeks to four pulls the opening forward by about three weeks, not five, because these four durations do not compress at all.

GateStartsClearsWhat it is
App verificationSubmitted 12 OctoberMid-to-late November Google’s review of the sensitive scope. Until it lands, a hundred people in the lifetime of the project may connect an account, and two are already used. It is the longest wait in the project and it is already running.

A week of recommendations reviewed26 October6 November The requirements’ gate for phase 2: the client’s Google Ads consultant checks real proposals for accuracy before anything is allowed to apply one.
Standard API access6 NovemberLate November to mid-December Ten to fifteen business days after applying, often longer, and the application needs week four’s features to exist. Required before serving real customers at scale.
Thirty days of pilot writes6 November6 December The requirements’ gate for phase 3: a month of real changes with zero guardrail breaches and every one revertible. It cannot be shortened by working faster, only by deciding to launch on less evidence.

So two dates matter, not one. The core is complete on 6 November and the last of the requirements on 20 November: written, tested and deployed, usable by you and by pilot accounts the same day. Opening to paying customers waits on Google’s reviews and on the thirty-day write gate, which ends on 6 December — so the realistic date is mid-December 2026. Launching on less evidence than that gate is a decision available to the client; it is not one I would make quietly on their behalf.

What the client needs to decide, and by when

At this pace a decision is the most expensive thing in the plan, because a week of waiting is two milestones. Compressing nine weeks into four moves every one of these dates forward by about three weeks, and that — more than any amount of code — is what the four-week plan asks of the client.

What would break this plan, and what to do about it

Four weeks is the two-month plan with the room taken out. Every risk below was survivable before; now each one costs the end date unless something gives, and the honest answer is to say in advance what gives: the fortnight after 6 November absorbs it first, and the core four weeks hold.

RiskCostsWhat to do
A decision takes a fortnight1–2 weeks The two that matter are needed by 23 October. If an answer is not ready, say so and I will build the default behind a setting rather than stop — but the default then has to be lived with or redone, and redoing it is a week.
Google refuses or queries something1–4 weeks Verification is already submitted; Standard access goes in on 6 November. A query usually needs a same-day answer, so whoever owns the Google account should be reachable through November.
The pace does not doubleup to 4 weeks This is the plan’s central bet. The fallback is not a failure but the previous plan: one milestone a week puts the core at 4 December. I will say which week it slipped in the week it slips, not at the end.
Scope growsa week each Everything in both documents is in the plan and there is no slack left. Anything new now displaces something dated, and the honest thing is to name which, at the time.
Review arrives lateup to 2 weeks Each week ends deployed and testable. At two milestones a week, feedback on week one arriving in week four is twice as expensive as it was in the old plan.

If four weeks has to hold whatever happens

  • Hold the chat back. Otto’s inbox is the product; the conversation is the demonstration of it. Deferring it saves about three days in week four and costs no capability.
  • Bulk actions after launch. One change at a time is the whole requirement for a small business with two campaigns. Saves two days in week four.
  • Agency last. Ten client accounts, per-client fees and white labelling are a plan, not a feature of the core. Already outside the four weeks, in week six.

The one thing I would not cut is the change pipeline in week three: the proposed change, the guardrails, the undo and the append-only log. It is tempting, with a month instead of two, to apply the first recommendation directly and add the record-keeping afterwards. Every change made before that exists would be outside the system that makes this product safe to sell, and the client’s own rule — nothing changes without a decision — would be a promise with nothing behind it. If the pace does not hold, I will move the date before I move that.
