PPCway delivery plan

Every requirement in the Developer Requirements document and the customer dashboard handoff, set against what is already built and running, with a date for each milestone and the reasoning behind it.

The build is planned over four weeks, at the pace this project has actually been running. Two things cannot be compressed by working faster: Google’s reviews, and the soak periods the requirements themselves set as exit gates. Both are named and dated separately, so the plan does not pretend they are work.

Running today, at app.ppcway.com

  • The free audit, which is the whole acquisition funnel: fourteen public checks on the Google listing, the website and the competition, with a real crawler, a headless browser and a live mobile speed test.
  • Google’s own search volumes turned into the money line — "about 11,280 searches a month for what you do in Oakville, $18 to $53 a click" — shown to visitors who have not signed up.
  • Google sign-in, with an audit run before signing up following the person into their account.
  • A read-only Google Ads connection: the owner grants it, picks which account we read, and we read spend, search terms, Quality Score, impression share and ad policy status.
  • The dashboard in the client’s design: home, campaigns and one campaign in full, reports, get started, the audit and three working calculators.

One caution when judging progress by looking at the app. Some screens from the earlier design are still drawn with sample data rather than wired to anything. Every screen in the new design is either reading live data or says plainly that its machinery is not built yet. No screen shows invented numbers as though they were the merchant’s.

How the dates were reached

This is not a conventional estimate, because it is not a conventional pace. In four weeks the project has produced the audit engine, the Google Ads integration, the deployment, the domain, the client’s dashboard and the live account reading — and the last of those took a single day. The plan below assumes that continues: roughly one milestone a week rather than one feature.

What it does not assume is that anything outside the keyboard moves faster. Google reviews take the time Google takes. A seven-day sync with no mismatches takes seven days. Thirty days of pilot writes takes thirty days. Those are on a separate track, and they are why the build finishing in November does not mean customers on the same day.

AssumptionWhat it means for the dates
The same pace as the last four weeksA milestone a week. If it drops to a conventional single-developer pace, the build runs to roughly June 2027 instead — the difference is that large, and it is the main risk in this plan.
You review as it lands, not at the endEach week ends with something deployed and testable. A week’s worth of feedback arriving in week four costs more than a week to unpick.
Google’s reviews are not oursApp verification (weeks) and Standard access (10–15 business days, often longer) are started as early as possible and run behind the build.
The decisions arrive before their weekThey block week three. At this pace a decision that takes a fortnight is the single most expensive thing in the plan.
Scope does not growEverything in the requirements and the handoff is in. Anything new displaces something, and the document says what.
Testing is in the estimateEvery figure includes writing the tests, not just the feature. That is why they look slower than a demo would.

The milestones

Friday 17 October 2026

Week 1 · Who everybody is, and the clock on Google started

  • Organisations, memberships and roles. Owner, admin, member, expert and client viewer, enforced in the backend rather than hidden in the interface. Everything later hangs off this: plans, agencies, and who may approve a change.
  • The connect flow in full. The handoff’s Get started screen, both paths: connect, choose accounts, watch the first sync stream in, land on a first look.
  • Email and password sign-up with verification, beside the Google button.
  • Disconnecting, from a screen rather than only in the code, with the stored permission deleted.

Submitted on day one, because the clock is Google’s: app verification, which lifts the hundred-user limit. Nothing in the build waits for it.

Friday 24 October 2026

Week 2 · The local mirror

  • Campaigns, ad groups, keywords, ads and daily performance facts held in our own database, with the Google resource name and the version we last saw.
  • The sync: structure every fifteen minutes, performance hourly for today, and a nightly pass over the previous three days to catch late conversions. Manual refresh on demand.
  • External change detection: anything changed directly in Google Ads is noticed, recorded as "made in Google Ads", and does not get silently overwritten.

Why a week of its own: every screen after this reads from the mirror rather than from Google, which is what makes the two-second dashboard in the requirements possible at all.

Gate starts: seven days of clean sync on five internal accounts.

Friday 31 October 2026

Week 3 · The dashboard, for real

  • Home against the mirror: the results strip with the change against the previous period, "needs you", the Otto activity list, and the Visibility Score over time.
  • Campaigns and campaign detail finished: the per-row sparkline, the Learning chip counting its days, and all five tabs.
  • Reports: thirty days, ninety days and year on year, the significance test that decides "a real change" from "within the normal range", the definitions panel, and sharing by an expiring link.

You can see at the end of the week: the handoff’s home screen with Byte9’s own numbers in it, loading from our database.

Friday 7 November 2026

Week 4 · The audit becomes a living tool, and the post starts arriving

  • Scheduled re-runs and change detection: website checks daily, listing checks weekly, ads checks with each sync, and a re-check within an hour of a page changing.
  • The history: the score over time with markers where fixes landed, "what changed since last time", and every past run kept.
  • Per-page landing checks on every page the ads send people to, not only the home page.
  • Email: the transactional provider, the weekly plain-language summary every Monday, the alerts, and "send the fix list to my web person" with a link that needs no login.
Friday 14 November 2026

Week 5 · Otto, watching but not touching

  • The recommendation engine: a daily pass over each account that turns findings into proposals — wasteful search terms, weak keywords, budget lost to a cap, a disapproved ad, a bidding strategy with enough data to change — each with a plain reason, the evidence, an expected impact and an undo plan.
  • The inbox and the cards from the handoff: the lifecycle bar, the evidence list, the before-and-after table, and the result when a change is measured.
  • The language model behind a server-side service, with prompt logging and policy filtering, writing words only. Every number in a card comes from the account, not the model.

Nothing is applied. This is the week the product starts having opinions, and the week to check they are good ones before it can act on them.

Exit gate: the client’s Google Ads consultant reviews a week of real recommendations for accuracy.

Friday 21 November 2026

Week 6 · The change pipeline and the guardrails

  • Proposed change and applied change as first-class records: type, target, before, after, reason, evidence, risk, route, status — immutable once applied, which is what powers both the history and the undo.
  • Guardrails, checked before every write in a fixed order: daily and monthly spend caps, the largest budget move allowed in a day, blocked keywords, protected brand terms, and no changes inside the fourteen-day learning window except fixes.
  • The write path to Google: one service, batched mutates, partial-failure handling, a per-account queue with retries and backoff, and an alert at 80% of the daily quota. A failed write never leaves half a change behind.
  • Thresholds as settings, so an owner can tighten or loosen the routes within what their plan allows.

Needs two decisions by Friday 13 November: which change types may auto-apply and whether auto-apply is on for a new account, and the data residency question.

Friday 28 November 2026

Week 7 · Approving, undoing, and the expert

  • The three routes: auto-applied and reported, one tap by the owner, and expert sign-off before the owner sees it.
  • Undo: the toast with its six seconds, and thirty days to revert anything from the change history, with the revert itself logged as a change.
  • Editing from the dashboard: pause, resume, the budget preview card with its three options, a negative keyword in one click from a wasteful search term, and bulk actions.
  • The expert console: the queue of assigned accounts, sign-off with a note the customer sees, booked sessions, and hours logged against the plan allowance.
  • Chat with Otto: the tools that let it read the account, and a proposed change card that can only become a change through the same approval as everything else.

Gate starts: thirty days of pilot writes with zero guardrail breaches.

Applied this week: Standard API access, once the required features exist.

Friday 5 December 2026

Week 8 · New advertisers

  • The PPCway manager account and account creation: a client account in the customer’s currency and time zone, the customer invited as admin so they own it, and the guided deep link into Google’s billing, with launch blocked until billing is active.
  • The campaign builder, all eight steps: goal, services, where and when, keywords with real estimates from Keyword Planner, ads with a live preview and a policy check, budget and bidding, tracking, then review and launch — autosaving at every step, and writing through week six’s pipeline rather than around it.
  • Learning mode: the fourteen to twenty-one day window, the day-3 and day-7 check-ins, and the rule that no bids move inside it.
Friday 11 December 2026

Week 9 · Plans, the phone, and everything found by using it

  • Plans, limits and billing: Starter, Growth and Agency with their account, spend and expert-hour limits enforced in the backend, the per-currency price book, the subscription, and the warnings at 80% and 100% of managed spend.
  • The phone layout, which the handoff designs separately: a tab bar, a floating Ask Otto, and approvals that work with a thumb.
  • Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA, localisation of currency and time zone with the interface ready for twelve languages, traces across the gateway and a status page.
  • A security and privacy pass: the data processing agreement, automated deletion within thirty days of a request, staff multi-factor sign-in, and the client secret rotated.
  • The handoff’s QA list: the same numbers on every screen for the same range, approve then undo restoring both the account and the badge counts, a budget rise of 50% routing to the expert, the builder surviving a reload, the streams recovering from a dropped connection, and every screen against its screenshot at 1440 pixels.

At the end of this week the build is complete — every requirement in both documents written, tested and deployed.

What the build cannot buy, however fast it goes

The requirements set exit gates that are measured in calendar days, and Google sets review times nobody can hurry. Both run behind the build rather than inside it, and they are the reason the build finishing on 13 November is not the same as opening the doors.

GateStartsClearsWhat it is
App verification13 OctoberMid-to-late November Google’s review of the sensitive scope. Until it lands, a hundred people in the lifetime of the project may connect an account, and two are already used. Submitted on day one for exactly this reason.
Standard API access24 NovemberMid-to-late December Ten to fifteen business days after applying, and the application needs week seven’s features to exist. Required before serving real customers at scale.
Seven days of clean sync24 October31 October The requirements’ own gate for phase 1: five internal accounts synced for a week with no data mismatches. It runs quietly underneath week three.
Thirty days of pilot writes28 November28 December The requirements’ gate for phase 3: a month of real changes with zero guardrail breaches and every one revertible. It cannot be shortened by working faster, only by deciding to launch on less evidence.

So two dates matter, not one. The build is complete on 11 December: everything in both documents, written, tested and deployed, usable by you and by pilot accounts the same day. Opening to paying customers waits on Google’s reviews and on the thirty-day write gate, which ends on 28 December — so the realistic date is early January 2027. Launching on less evidence than that gate is a decision available to the client; it is not one I would make quietly on their behalf.

What would break this plan, and what to do about it

Two months is comfortable at the pace this project has been running — it is a month of work with a month of room. These are the four things that would spend that room, in the order they are likely.

RiskCostsWhat to do
A decision takes a fortnight1–2 weeks The two that matter most are needed by 13 November. If an answer is not ready, say so and I will build the default behind a setting rather than stop — but the default then has to be lived with or redone.
Google refuses or queries something1–4 weeks Both reviews are submitted as early as the work allows. A query usually needs a same-day answer, so whoever owns the Google account should be reachable through November and December.
Scope growsa week each Everything in both documents is in the plan. Anything new displaces something, and the honest thing is to name which, at the time, rather than absorb it quietly.
Review arrives lateup to a week Each week ends deployed and testable. Feedback on week two arriving in week eight is the most expensive kind, because six weeks are built on top of it.

If it has to be shorter still

  • Launch for existing advertisers only. Weeks 1 to 7 are a complete product for a business that already runs Google Ads: it audits, recommends, and changes with approval. Week eight exists for businesses with no account at all. Dropping it brings the build in on 28 November.
  • Hold the chat back. Otto’s inbox is the product; the conversation is the demonstration of it. Deferring saves about three days and no capability.
  • Agency last. Ten client accounts, per-client fees and white labelling are a plan, not a feature of the core. Deferring saves about three days in week nine.

The one thing I would not cut is the change pipeline in week six: the proposed change, the guardrails, the undo and the append-only log. It is tempting to apply the first recommendation directly and add the record-keeping afterwards. Every change made before that exists would be outside the system that makes this product safe to sell, and the client’s own rule — nothing changes without a decision — would be a promise with nothing behind it.

The pace is the one this project has been running at: a single day last week produced the whole dashboard in the client’s design, two new reporting endpoints and three fixes found by testing against a live account. The plan assumes that continues, and says plainly where it would break.
