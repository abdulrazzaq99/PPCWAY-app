PPCway delivery plan

Every requirement in the Developer Requirements document and the customer dashboard handoff, set against what is already built and running, with a date for each milestone and the reasoning behind it.

The dates assume one developer. Where a wait is Google’s rather than ours — app verification, Standard API access — it is named and placed early, because those reviews take weeks and nothing we do makes them faster.

Running today, at app.ppcway.com

  • The free audit, which is the whole acquisition funnel: fourteen public checks on the Google listing, the website and the competition, with a real crawler, a headless browser and a live mobile speed test.
  • Google’s own search volumes turned into the money line — "about 11,280 searches a month for what you do in Oakville, $18 to $53 a click" — shown to visitors who have not signed up.
  • Google sign-in, with an audit run before signing up following the person into their account.
  • A read-only Google Ads connection: the owner grants it, picks which account we read, and we read spend, search terms, Quality Score, impression share and ad policy status.
  • The dashboard in the client’s design: home, campaigns and one campaign in full, reports, get started, the audit and three working calculators.
  • The platform underneath: a domain with a managed certificate, both services in Toronto, secrets in a manager, refresh tokens encrypted, 562 tests.

One caution when judging progress by looking at the app. Some screens from the earlier design are still drawn with sample data rather than wired to anything. Every screen in the new design is either reading live data or says plainly that its machinery is not built yet. No screen shows invented numbers as though they were the merchant’s.

How the dates were reached

Four weeks of work produced the audit engine, the Google Ads integration, the deployment and the dashboard shell. The estimates below assume the same pace: roughly one substantial feature a week for one developer, less where the work waits on Google.

AssumptionWhat it means for the dates
One developerMilestones run one after another. Two developers on independent tracks compresses the plan to roughly mid-March 2027; the second column of every milestone says what can run in parallel.
Google’s reviews are not oursApp verification (weeks) and Standard access (10–15 business days, often longer) are started as early as possible and run behind the build.
The client answers on the dayThree open questions block milestone 3. Each week they go unanswered moves everything after them by a week.
No holidays assumed beyond two weeksLate December is treated as two weeks of no progress. Any other absence adds to the end.
Testing is in the estimateEvery figure includes writing the tests, not just the feature. That is why they look slower than a demo would.

The milestones

Friday 7 November 2026

M1 · Foundation complete

Four weeks. Finishes phase 1 and its exit gate.

  • Organisations, memberships and roles (1 week). Owner, admin, member, expert and client viewer, enforced in the backend rather than hidden in the interface. Everything later hangs off this: plans, agencies, who may approve.
  • The local mirror and its sync (2 weeks). Campaigns, ad groups, keywords, ads and daily performance facts, synced every fifteen minutes for structure and hourly for performance, with a nightly backfill for late conversions. The dashboard then reads our own database, which is what makes a two-second home screen possible.
  • The connect flow in full (1 week). The handoff’s Get started screen: connect, choose accounts, watch the first sync run, land on a first look at the account.

Exit gate: five internal accounts connected and synced for seven days with no data mismatches, which is the requirement’s own test.

Started in parallel: the app verification submission to Google, because it is slow and nothing depends on finishing it.

Friday 16 January 2027

M2 · Read and recommend

Nine weeks, including two weeks of no progress over the holidays.

  • Dashboard home against the mirror (1 week). The results strip, "needs you", the Otto activity list and the Visibility Score over time, all reading local data.
  • Campaigns and campaign detail (1.5 weeks). The filter, the sparkline per row, and the five tabs: overview, keywords, search terms, ads and change history.
  • Reports (1.5 weeks). Thirty, ninety days and year on year, the significance test that decides "a real change" from "within the normal range", definitions, and sharing.
  • The audit as a living tool (1.5 weeks). Scheduled re-runs, change detection, the score history, "what changed since last time", per-page landing checks and the alerts.
  • Otto’s recommendation engine, read only (3 weeks). The daily pass over each account that turns findings into proposals with a reason, the evidence, an expected impact and an undo plan. Shown in the inbox; nothing is applied.
  • Email (0.5 week). The transactional provider, the weekly plain-language summary and the alerts, which several requirements depend on.

Exit gate: pilot customers use it weekly, and the recommendations are reviewed for accuracy by the client’s Google Ads consultant before any of them can be applied.

Friday 19 March 2027

M3 · Approve and write

Nine weeks. The heart of the product, and the first time PPCway changes anything.

  • The change pipeline (2 weeks). Proposed change and applied change as first-class records: type, target, before, after, reason, evidence, risk, route, status. Immutable once applied, which is what powers history and undo.
  • Guardrails (1.5 weeks). Checked before every write, in a fixed order: daily and monthly spend caps, the maximum budget move in a day, blocked keywords, protected brand terms, and no changes during the fourteen-day learning window except fixes.
  • The write path to Google (2 weeks). One service, batched mutates, partial-failure handling, a per-account queue with retries and backoff, and an alert at 80% of the daily quota. A failed write never leaves half a change behind.
  • Approvals and undo (1.5 weeks). The three routes — auto-applied, one tap, expert sign-off — the toast with undo, and thirty days to revert any change from the history.
  • Editing campaigns from the dashboard (1 week). Pause, resume, budget with its preview card, negative keywords in one click from a search term, bulk actions.
  • The expert console (1 week). The queue of assigned accounts, sign-off with a note the customer sees, sessions, and hours logged against the plan allowance.

Exit gate: thirty days of pilot writes with zero guardrail breaches and every change revertible — again, the requirement’s own test.

Blocked by: three client decisions, listed at the end of this document.

Friday 28 May 2027

M4 · New advertisers

Ten weeks. Can start before M3 finishes, since the builder writes through M3’s pipeline.

  • The PPCway manager account and account creation (2 weeks). Creating a client account in the customer’s currency and time zone, inviting them as an admin so they own it, and the guided deep link into Google’s billing with a block on launching until it is active.
  • The campaign builder, eight steps (4 weeks). Goal, services, where and when, keywords with estimates, ads with a live preview and a policy check, budget and bidding, tracking, then review and launch. Autosave at every step.
  • Learning mode (1 week). The fourteen to twenty-one day window, the day-3 and day-7 check-ins, and the rule that no bids change inside it.
  • Plans, limits and billing (2 weeks). Starter, Growth and Agency with their account, spend and expert-hour limits enforced in the backend, the per-currency price book, the subscription, and the warnings at 80% and 100% of a plan’s managed spend.
  • Standard access and the required features (1 week of work, plus Google’s review). Target ROAS bidding and the search terms view must ship whether or not launch customers need them, because Google audits for them.

Exit gate: the first ten new advertisers launched end to end, and Standard access approved.

Friday 25 June 2027

M5 · Hardening and launch

Four weeks. The work that is invisible until it is missing.

  • The phone layout (1.5 weeks). The handoff designs it separately: a tab bar, a floating Ask Otto, and approvals that work with a thumb.
  • Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA (1 week). Full keyboard operation, screen-reader labels on every chart and table, visible focus, and colour never the only signal.
  • Localisation (0.5 week). Account currency and time zone everywhere, and the interface strings ready for the twelve site languages, English first.
  • Observability and the status page (0.5 week). Traces across the gateway, and components for the app, the sync, Otto and the Google connection.
  • A security and privacy pass (0.5 week). The data processing agreement, automated deletion within thirty days of a request, and staff multi-factor sign-in.

If the plan needs to be shorter

The honest levers, in the order I would pull them.

  • A second developer takes the independent track. The builder, billing and the expert console barely touch the change pipeline, so two people working in parallel bring launch to roughly mid-March 2027. This is the only lever that does not cut scope.
  • Launch for existing advertisers first. Milestones 1 to 3 are a complete product for a business that already runs Google Ads: it audits, recommends, and changes with approval. Phase 4 exists for businesses with no account at all. Dropping it from the first release moves launch to late March 2027.
  • Hold the chat back. Otto’s inbox is the product; the conversational interface is the demonstration. Deferring it saves about two weeks and no capability.
  • Agency last. Ten client accounts, per-client fees and white labelling are a plan, not a feature of the core. Deferring saves about two weeks in M4.

The one thing I would not cut is the change pipeline in M3: proposed change, guardrails, undo and the append-only log. It is tempting to apply the first recommendation directly and add the record-keeping later. Every change after that would then be outside the system that makes this product safe to sell, and the client’s own rule — nothing changes without a decision — would be a promise with nothing behind it.
